# Vision

dac8.org exists to challenge indiscriminate crypto-asset reporting and the
durable financial surveillance it enables. Bitcoin Austria is preparing
concrete Austrian cases along the data-protection route and examining every
other supportable legal avenue. Austria is the starting point rather than the
boundary.

Public awareness is an independent campaign goal. Readers should understand
why financial privacy matters in their own lives, recognize the risks of
excessive data collection, and see concrete ways to respond. The campaign brings
people and organizations with shared concerns together and gives those concerns
a public voice. Its value includes informed debate and collective action,
alongside the legal effort to establish effective limits on surveillance.

The campaign should help credible partners carry the work into other European
countries. As those initiatives emerge, the website should become multilingual
and share arguments, primary sources, and practical experience without
pretending that one country's procedure can simply be copied into another.

We intend to use every available lawful avenue. National proceedings must fit
their jurisdiction; where EU law becomes decisive, the campaign should pursue
the procedural routes that can bring those questions before the Court of
Justice of the European Union. We do not promise referrals, judgments, or
timelines that the campaign cannot control.

The prospects of winning are uncertain. Our optimism rests on the ability to
act together, make the risks visible, and challenge intrusive rules. Do not
present confidence in a favorable judgment as the reason to support the campaign.
Raising awareness matters in its own right; pursuing effective legal protection
remains a concrete goal, not a symbolic exercise.

The audience includes affected individuals and businesses, legal and technical
experts, partner organizations, supporters, and anyone who needs a verifiable
account of what DAC8 requires. The campaign defends financial privacy and autonomy
and rejects mass surveillance. The state must justify intrusions into private
life; invoking money laundering or tax evasion does not replace that justification.
Investigations should target specific suspicions of crime, with a legal basis
and independent oversight. Uninvolved people must be protected from the risks of
indiscriminate data collection, including breaches, false suspicion and abuse.
The campaign does not endorse taxation or anti-money-laundering policy. Express
these principles through the purpose of each page rather than repeating a
manifesto or adding defensive disclaimers. Legal credibility, accurate procedural
status, and primary-source evidence take priority over rhetorical reach.

## Editorial principles

Apply these principles to public pages, translations, calls to action, search
descriptions and sharing previews:

- Lead with the reader's stake: financial information can reveal private choices
  and expose people to profiling, abuse and physical harm. Explain the connection
  plainly, using supported examples without exaggerating or minimizing risks.
- Be resolute, accessible and grounded. Frame financial privacy as a necessity
  for a free society and a liberal democracy. State our values without requiring
  readers to share a party affiliation or a broader political ideology.
- Express hope through concrete action and shared purpose. Acknowledge the open
  procedural outcome where relevant, then give readers a useful next step. Avoid
  repeating uncertainty throughout the site or replacing it with victory promises.
- Convey solidarity through the initiative, its people and its work. Avoid
  unsupported claims of widespread support and generic motivational slogans.
- Keep public copy focused on what readers need to understand or do. Research
  notes supply evidence, not the structure or wording of the page. Preserve
  distinctions that affect meaning, especially reporting versus retention, but
  omit internal deliberations and defensive caveats that add no useful context.
- Keep wording concise and natural in each language. Use clear headings and
  short action labels; avoid filler, repeated manifestos and em dashes. Carry the
  same goals across the site without repeating the same paragraph on every page.
